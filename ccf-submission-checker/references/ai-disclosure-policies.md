# Venue AI-Disclosure Policies (Official Sources Only)

> Compiled by ccf-submission-checker from official venue pages only.
> Every entry below was verified against the venue's own site on the
> stated access date; policy text is paraphrased, never copied from
> third-party compilations. Policies change between cycles — re-check
> the official page for the actual target cycle before sign-off.
>
> **Guard rule.** Before a submission is signed off, the checker must
> confirm that the manuscript's AI-use disclosure (its presence,
> placement, and wording) matches the target venue's *current* policy
> as stated on the official page — not this snapshot. A mismatch is a
> submission blocker of the same class as a missing checklist item.
> When the venue's rule is prohibition-based rather than
> disclosure-based, confirm instead that no prohibited content is
> present.

Access date for all verified entries: **2026-11-03**.

## NeurIPS

- **Rule:** Use of an LLM that is an important, original, or
  non-standard component of the core method must be described in the
  paper (experimental setup or an equivalent section). Routine
  spell-checking, grammar suggestions, and programming aids used for
  editing need not be documented. Authors are fully responsible for
  all content, including references; only humans may be authors.
- **Where:** Paper text (method/experimental-setup description) and
  the Paper Checklist, item 16 ("Declaration of LLM usage").
- **Official source:** https://nips.cc/Conferences/2025/LLM (LLM
  policy page) and the NeurIPS Paper Checklist.

## ICML

- **Rule:** Authors may use generative AI tools, including LLMs, to
  assist in writing or research, and take full responsibility for all
  content, including anything that might be construed as plagiarism
  or scientific misconduct. LLMs are not eligible for authorship.
  Prompt injection into the review process is strictly forbidden and
  results in desk rejection. The call for papers states no separate
  author-side disclosure-statement mandate; ICML's mandatory
  declaration machinery applies to reviewers (the Policy A / Policy B
  declaration in the reviewer console).
- **Where:** No author disclosure location is mandated in the CFP
  text checked; responsibility and authorship rules apply to the
  submission as a whole.
- **Official source:** https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers
  (see also https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/LLM-Policy for the
  reviewing-side policy).

## ICLR

- **Rule:** Authors must explicitly state how they used LLMs, both in
  the paper's text and in the submission form. The paper must contain
  a dedicated AI-use section that does not count toward the page
  limit; the policy page provides boilerplate wording and a subtask
  table marking uses as Required vs Recommended for disclosure
  (Required includes, among others: synthetic datasets, theoretical
  models or conceptual frameworks, formulating or proving
  mathematical claims, hypotheses, methodology and experiment-design
  feedback, implementing methods, translation, data cleaning,
  qualitative analysis, and interpreting results). Authors are
  ultimately responsible for LLM-produced content; falsehood,
  plagiarism, or misrepresentation via LLMs violates the Code of
  Ethics and may lead to desk rejection.
- **Where:** Paper text (mandatory AI-use section, outside the page
  limit) **and** the submission form.
- **Official source:** https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors

## ACL (including ARR venues)

- **Rule:** Generative AI tools may not be listed as authors of a
  submission, and any use of generative AI tools to create content
  should be fully disclosed in the Acknowledgements section (the
  policy's example: "Section 3 was written with inputs from
  ChatGPT."). The policy builds on the ACL 2023 Policy on AI Writing
  Assistance.
- **Where:** Paper text, Acknowledgements section.
- **Official source:** ACL Policy on Publication Ethics (approved by
  the ACL Executive Committee 2024-06-15),
  https://aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php?title=ACL_Policy_on_Publication_Ethics

## AAAI

- **Rule:** Prohibition-based rather than disclosure-based. Papers
  that include text generated by a large-scale language model such
  as ChatGPT are prohibited unless the produced text is presented as
  part of the paper's experimental analysis. Using LLMs for editing
  or polishing author-written text is not prohibited. AI systems do
  not satisfy authorship criteria and cannot be used as a citable
  source; authors assume full responsibility, including plagiarism
  and veracity checks.
- **Where:** No disclosure location — compliance is about the
  content itself; LLM-generated material may appear only as
  experimental-analysis content.
- **Official source:** https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-26/main-technical-track-call/
  and https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-26/policies-for-aaai-26-authors/

## IJCAI

- **Rule:** LLMs do not satisfy authorship criteria. Authors who use
  an LLM in any part of the paper-writing process assume full
  responsibility for all content, including plagiarism and
  correctness checking. LLM-generated text used as part of the
  paper's methodology or experimental analysis is allowed but must
  be properly documented and described in the paper. The submission
  FAQ adds that LLMs may be used to polish style and language, warns
  that LLM detection tools are employed, and states that papers
  found to be written by LLMs will be desk rejected.
- **Where:** Paper text (documentation of any methodological or
  experimental LLM use); no separate disclosure form is specified in
  the sources checked.
- **Official source:** https://2026.ijcai.org/ijcai-ecai-2026-call-for-papers-demos/
  (Use of LLMs clause) and the official IJCAI-ECAI 2026 submission
  FAQ, item S22.

## CVPR

- **Rule:** The author guidelines' FAQ states that authors may use
  any tools they find productive; authors are responsible for any
  misrepresentation, factual inaccuracy, or plagiarism, and papers
  containing citations of non-existent material will be rejected
  (possibly without review). "An LLM did it" is explicitly not a
  defence. No explicit author disclosure-statement mandate was found
  in the guidelines text checked — record exactly that, and re-check
  for the target cycle.
- **Where:** No mandated disclosure location found in the checked
  guidelines.
- **Official source:** https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines

## Nature (Nature Portfolio journals)

- **Rule:** LLMs such as ChatGPT do not satisfy authorship criteria,
  because authorship carries accountability that cannot be applied
  to LLMs. Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the
  Methods section (or a suitable alternative section when the article
  has no Methods). Use of an LLM or other AI tool for "AI assisted
  copy editing" does not need to be declared. Human authors remain
  accountable for the final text in all cases.
- **Where:** Paper text, Methods section (or equivalent); copy
  editing use is exempt.
- **Official source:** https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai
