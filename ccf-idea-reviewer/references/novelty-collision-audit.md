# Testing a novelty claim against prior work

State the particular contribution being claimed. A proposed operation, a theorem about that operation and its use under a new assumption can have different precedents. Use the closest work already identified in the [idea review](strict-idea-review.md).

| Compare | Evidence that can decide the question |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | What quantity or failure does each work address? |
| Mechanism | Which inputs, information and operations does each method use? Anchor equivalence or a material difference in the original method, equation or proof. |
| Claim | What consequence did the prior authors actually derive or observe? Locate their claim and distinguish it from the proposed prediction. |
| Boundary | Under which assumptions, population, budget or access conditions does each claim hold? Explain how a changed condition affects the comparison. |

For example, a paper that estimates a score from paired observations at training time may anticipate the estimator. A proposal to recover it from unpaired observations adds a distinct information constraint. Whether the cited method already handles that constraint depends on its derivation. An abstract that only names the score leaves this question open.

In the existing review, identify the source version and decisive passage, then state exactly which part was anticipated. If a difference survives, describe the condition or prediction that would distinguish it. A prior method alone can settle a claim to have introduced that operation, even when the prior authors use different terminology. Where the decisive passage is unavailable, keep the judgment provisional and identify what would settle it. Bound any finding of no overlap to the inspected sources, including supplied-only coverage when browsing is excluded.
