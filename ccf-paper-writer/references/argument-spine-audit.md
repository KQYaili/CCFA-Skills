# Argument Spine And Reverse-Outline Audit

Use for a full manuscript, a disjoint Introduction/Discussion, or a substantive structural rewrite **after** the existing storyline and materials-to-manuscript guidance. Skip for a local sentence edit, ordinary copyedit, or an otherwise coherent short section. This is an optional writing diagnostic, not a new Skill, worksheet quota, or mandatory pre-draft gate.

## Recover The Governing Claim

State, in language justified by supplied materials:

- The scientific question, setting and closest serious alternative;
- The unresolved distinction or falsifiable obstacle;
- The contribution **promise** (what the method/proof/resource actually changes), distinct from the **motivation** (why it matters);
- The decisive evidence or proof that could support, narrow, or contradict the promise;
- The boundary the reader should remember.

Do not manufacture a failing baseline, a positive effect, or a motivation that was not established. A paper about a negative diagnostic, theorem, dataset, or measurement needs its own argumentative structure; do not force a "failed method then successful fix" story.

## Reverse Outline Only The Units At Risk

For a substantial draft, walk the manuscript **in its current order** and assign each meaningful paragraph group, claim-bearing figure, or subsection one job:

| Unit or existing anchor | Reader question | Contribution or claim advanced | Evidence/proof or pending source | Link to preceding/next unit | Repair, if needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Useful unit jobs: establish setting; isolate a prior-work assumption; state an observation; define an object; derive an operation; compare evidence; explain a mechanism; bound a claim; interpret an exception. A group may do multiple jobs if clear; split it only when the scientific reasoning requires separation. Existing notes, `fig:*` slots and bibliography keys are the canonical references; don't create a second ledger merely to satisfy the table.

Flag only substantive defects:
1. **Motivation drift**: the paragraph describes a more ambitious problem than the method/tests address.
2. **Promise without operation**: a module or buzzword appears where a causal/computational distinction is required.
3. **Evidence displacement**: the claim is far from its proof, experiment, or appropriately scoped citation.
4. **False continuity**: a transition implies that one result explains another without a justified link.
5. **Conclusion inflation**: an ablation, simulation, or exploratory observation is stated as broad deployment superiority.

If a scientific assertion is unverified, route it to the existing [claim-promotion gate](../../ccf-common/references/claim-promotion-gate.md); do not polish an unknown into apparent certainty.

## Repairs And Stop Rule

Prioritize the smallest set of unit moves that fixes the reader's inference: reorder, shorten, add a supported bridge, narrow the claim, or move a comparison beside its evidence. Preserve actual negative/null findings. A revised figure caption must retain its stable `fig:*` identity and obey the [figure lifecycle](../../ccf-common/references/research-figure-lifecycle.md). After revising, reread the affected transitions once; do not force a second complete rewrite if the argument now holds.

Deliver the revised manuscript/section or a compact unit-level critique in the requested form. The audit does not authorize new experiments, model calls, a fixed citation target, or new standalone method figures.
